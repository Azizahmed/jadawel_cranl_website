# Jadawel identity system

This is the authority for visual decisions. The packaged master logo and these rules define version 1.0 of the approved identity.

## Brand core

- **Arabic name:** جداول
- **Brand idea:** من البيانات تُبنى الإمكانات
- **Positioning:** الطريق الموثوق والبسيط لتحويل البيانات المتفرقة إلى أنظمة عمل تبنيها الفرق بنفسها.
- **Traits:** موثوقة، سعودية، سهلة، مرنة، قوية تقنيًا، عربية أصيلة، حديثة.
- **Visual principle:** a clear, confident mass; deliberate open space; and a restrained blue accent that guides attention.

The identity should feel operational and modern rather than decorative. It should show that Jadawel helps teams turn scattered data into usable systems.

## Approved logo

The approved logo is the complete Arabic wordmark in the packaged files. The black wordmark is the default. The white wordmark is the reverse version for blue or dark backgrounds.

- Preserve the artwork, proportions, internal spacing, curves, and diamond dot.
- Keep a clear space of at least **X** on every side, where **X equals the height of the diamond dot**.
- Recommended minimum width: **120 px** in digital use and **30 mm** in print.
- Increase the size whenever the diamond, counters, or internal gaps lose clarity.
- Place the black logo only on white, cloud, or another demonstrably high-contrast light field.
- Place the white logo on Jadawel Blue or Jadawel Ink.

Logo integrity means using the supplied file rather than typesetting the name. Keep the wordmark one color. Preserve aspect ratio. Keep effects, gradients, shadows, outlines, masks, and decorative containers away from the logo.

The diamond may support layouts as a focus marker, list device, data point, or compositional accent. It does not replace the full logo and is not an approved app icon.

## Color system

CMYK values are production approximations. Confirm coated/uncoated profiles and physical proofs with the printer before a high-volume print run.

Use an approximate overall balance of **60% light space, 25% ink, and 15% blue**. Marketing campaigns may increase blue. Dense product tables and data views should reduce it to protect readability. Mint, amber, and coral communicate states; they do not become alternate brand primaries.

## Typography

Use **Thmanyah Sans** for Arabic and Latin text when licensed and available.

- Black or Bold: major headings and decisive statements.
- Bold: section headings and product titles.
- Medium: labels, controls, metadata, and data emphasis.
- Regular: body copy, explanatory text, and long-form reading.

Use sentence case in English. Keep Arabic headings factual and direct. Avoid ornamental display typography. A safe fallback stack is `"Thmanyah Sans", "Noto Sans Arabic", Arial, sans-serif`, but a fallback is a disclosed production exception, not a replacement identity.

## Composition

- Start Arabic layouts from the right and use a six-column grid when the format permits.
- Use generous margins, clear alignment axes, and a small number of strong masses.
- Use the vertical bar derived from the logo as a structural divider or emphasis block.
- Use the diamond sparingly as a focal marker.
- Keep hierarchy visible through size, weight, spacing, and color rather than decoration.
- Prefer one dominant message or product view per composition.

## Imagery

The product is the primary hero. Show interfaces, data structures, workflows, or tangible work outcomes clearly. When people are necessary, use credible Saudi work contexts and natural behavior. Avoid generic server rooms, padlocks, staged handshakes, floating holograms, or imagery that reduces data residency and security to clichés.

## Icons and data graphics

Icons are functional, simple, and consistent in stroke weight. Use filled treatment for active states and line treatment for ordinary states. The diamond can mark focus but should not appear inside every icon.

Data graphics prioritize explanation:

- Blue highlights one selected series or key value.
- Mint, amber, and coral communicate states.
- Labels, units, and sources remain visible.
- Avoid 3D charts, gradients, decorative depth, and color-only differentiation.
